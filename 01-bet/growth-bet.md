## Growth hypothesis

FinWise's biggest growth problem is FinWise's biggest growth problem is that paying customers churn faster than new ones convert (317 churned vs. 128 paid conversions over 13 months)., because because customers may not be getting enough ongoing value after they pay, and today the product's value rests on a single user's usage..

_Working notes: I think FinWise’s biggest problem is that paid customers are not experiencing enough ongoing value to justify the cost, leading to high churn. Evidence: One-year retention is only 40%, meaning 60% of paying customers leave within a year. That suggests the product may not be delivering enough sustained value after conversion.
Trial-to-paid conversion is only 2%, which may indicate users are not seeing a strong enough reason to continue paying after the reverse trial.
FinWise is already spending heavily on paid acquisition, but additional acquisition spend is no longer creating meaningful growth. That suggests the bigger issue may be further down the funnel, especially retention. Against the data: Trial-to-paid conversion is the biggest drop-off in the funnel, with 128 of 6,424 trials (1.99%) becoming paid. It is also remarkably stable, staying between 1.87% and 2.08% every month, while visit-to-trial swings from 3.41% to 11.21%. This maps to the Revenue stage, not Retention, which is where my hypothesis sits. The data partly supports my hypothesis: churn exceeded new paid conversions in all 13 months (317 churned vs. 128 paid). It also challenges it. The 2% trial-to-paid rate describes users who haven't paid yet, so it can't show that paying customers lack ongoing value. And the highest-churn months (Apr, Jul, Sep 2024) had high engagement, with 7-9 sessions per user, which doesn't match a "low engagement drives churn" story. I'd update my thinking to: churn is real and worth investigating, but this dataset can't confirm why it happens._

_____

## The bet

the highest-leverage experiment I'd run first is a Collaboration loop: prompt owners to invite an accountant or partner after their first data import, then compare 1-year churn for invited and non-invited workspaces.

**Not doing:** I'm not going after more top-of-funnel traffic or trial-to-paid conversion tweaks because focusing there would pull effort from the bet above. Visits and trials moved independently in the data (Jun 2024 had the most visits and the fewest trials), so more traffic isn't a reliable lever, and the 2% trial-to-paid rate is stable month to month and doesn't tell me whether paying customers stay.

_____

## Growth loop

**Loop type:** Collaboration, I'd start with a Collaboration loop because shared workspaces tie into my retention hypothesis (more users per workspace means more ongoing value) while also generating new trials when collaborators sign up themselves.
